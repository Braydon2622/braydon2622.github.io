01AI for sales teams · Sales Cheat Code

Two problems my sales team had, and the AI I built to fix them.

Calls went sideways with no one there to coach, and reps lost hours a day deciding what to text. I built a coach for each, then trained the team until they used them.

I built these for my mortgage team, but the same approach works for any team that calls and texts leads: insurance, real estate, solar, auto or software sales.

Problem oneTexting · in daily use

Sales Text Cheat Code: a great reply to any text, then on autopilot

The problem: reps spent their mornings deciding what to text back and who to follow up with. Leads went cold from hesitation, not effort, and texts with words like "loan" or "refinance" got flagged on automated texting platforms like Bonzo.

  • Reads what's really going on. Hot, warm or cold, the stage of the deal, then the text to send with a two option close, a softer backup, the next step and when to follow up.
  • Replies on its own. When a client texts in, it writes the reply with the same playbook and sends it, or holds it for the loan officer to approve with one tap. Each weekday morning it also drafts the next text for every lead that's due, changing the angle by how many times they've been contacted.
  • Texts that get delivered. It says "home" instead of "loan," never quotes a rate or approval, and skips the words texting platforms like Bonzo flag.

Less time writing texts, more time on calls. Faster, better replies keep leads warm, and warm leads turn into closed loans.

In daily use by all 9 reps on my team. Every lead who says yes or sounds interested is flagged for the loan officer automatically.

Problem twoPhone calls · built and tested

Sales Call Cheat Code: training wheels for every call

The problem: new reps learn calls by making mistakes on real clients, and a manager can only listen to a few calls a week. Nobody tells the rep what they could have said until it's too late.

  • Coaches you during the call. When the client raises an objection or asks something tricky, a card shows what to say and what to ask next, about 1.6 seconds after they stop talking.
  • Knows who's talking. It tells the two voices apart by how they sound, so the coaching is about what the loan officer said, not the client.
  • Reviews every call after. What went well, what went wrong, what you could have said instead, the turning point, and a follow up text ready to send.
  • Shows where calls go wrong across the team, so coaching targets the real patterns instead of guesses.

Built and tested on 45 role play calls, 141 automated tests, about 2¢ per call. A pilot on my team's calls is waiting on company approval.

How I kept it safe, and how it got adopted

The loan officer stays in control

Automatic replies can wait for the loan officer's approval before anything goes out, and every call coaching card is a suggestion, not a script.

Lending rules built in

Every suggestion is checked before the rep sees it: no rate quotes, no approval promises, and opt outs honored right away.

Private by design

Call transcripts are scrubbed of personal details on import, and recordings aren't kept.

Adopted, not just built

Short enough to read on a phone, trained with situations from the team's own week, and simplified after watching what reps actually used.

TrainingThe other half of the job

I don't just hand people a tool. I teach them to use it.

I've run multiple hands on trainings for my loan officers. I take the team into a meeting room, put my screen on the TV, and walk through a real example live, so nobody has to guess what to do when they get back to their desk.

  1. Show it live.I run the tool on the big screen with a real example so everyone sees exactly what it does.
  2. Walk the exact steps.Where to click, what to paste, and what to do with the answer, one step at a time.
  3. Use their own situations.We practice on real scenarios from their week, so it sticks the next time a client texts.

ApproachThe first 90 days

How I'd roll out AI on your team.

The same order of operations I used with my own team. Tools change; this doesn't.

Days 1 to 30Listen and find the frictionShadow the people doing the work, time the manual steps, and pick the 2 or 3 tasks where people guess, repeat themselves or wait. Learn the data and privacy rules first and record a baseline.
Days 31 to 60Build small and pilotBuild simple tools with approved AI for those tasks. Pilot with a few early champions, train with their real situations, and cut anything people skip.
Days 61 to 90Roll out and measureShort hands on sessions for everyone, office hours and a shared prompt library. Report time saved, adoption and quality, then pick the next problem.

My rules

Privacy first. A person approves anything that reaches a customer. A simple tool that gets used beats a perfect one that doesn't.

What I measure

Hours saved per person, weekly active users, quality checks on AI output, and the business number the team already cares about.

Proof it works

On my own team I found reps losing their mornings to follow up texts. I built the coach, trained all 9 in hands on sessions, then automated it. All 9 use it daily, new texts get a reply within a minute, and every interested lead is flagged automatically.
